## Owner feedback, 2026-09-08

The owner likes the paired arcs and the resulting curved guidance and impacts.
The main question is whether using this form at every beat is necessary, and
whether single arcs or other coherent forms could sometimes be more suitable.
They welcome further performance research, do not regard the present result
as a ceiling, and do not require performance and variety to be solved at once.
Praise for an effect is context, not a request to hardcode more of that effect.
They prefer general mechanisms and physical feedback over scenario-specific
recipes or a prescribed mix of shapes.

Use **trajectory shaping** for the broad task and **paired-rail guidance** for
the current geometry. The distinction matters: the compiler searches entry,
turn, exit, support length, bias and offset using actual physics, but the
public configuration explicitly supplies an upper rail at fixed 12 px
clearance for every support. Pair selection has not emerged from a competition
with single arcs. The upper rail's shape follows the lower rail, and its
active span is not independently optimized.

Promising next experiments are to measure which upper-rail sections actually
matter through removal or shortening and full replay; then search rail coverage
and clearance alongside the existing curve controls. Removing all upper rails
ejects the audited River rider, but that does not establish that every upper
rail is necessary. Longer-horizon planning is another opportunity: current
search mostly chooses one interval at a time with an arrival objective and
limited backtracking.

These are hypotheses, not validated improvements or new acceptance rules.
The benchmark and scorer remain fixed. First measure the performance and
visual tradeoffs among viable geometries. If one form dominates the fixed
score, randomness alone does not establish a useful diversity policy; any
later creative selection among near-equivalent candidates should make its
quality tolerance explicit and remain separate from benchmark reporting.
