# Improving intermediate guide choices and transferring to facets

The owner authorized diagnosis, bounded search improvements, fresh evaluation,
and transfer to one existing geometry. Normal lines remain mandatory. Benchmark,
detector, scorer and physics are frozen. Guide count remains an optional artistic
preference, not an aesthetic score or a new specification control.

## Protocol fixed before development

The previous study's seeds 241–248 are now exposed evidence. Development uses
the original six passages and seeds 241/242, at 450,000 and 900,000 total physics
frames. The control is the previous recommended balanced two-path search with
independent guided/unguided references, frozen at `0f0da680`.

The diagnostic questions are whether later guide permissions undo simplification,
whether useful intermediate alternatives receive further search, and how much
construction capacity is lost to redundant or underfunded attempts. Controlled
ablations will compare preserving later permissions, a frontier spanning the
measured accuracy/guide-count range, and their combination. Geometry is still
independently searched; preserving permissions does not reuse an unvalidated ride.

Select the candidate on development results, then freeze it before running
**seeds 271–278**. The confirmation panel contains the original six passages
plus three newly authored passages, defined in
`scripts/gallery/guide_confirmation_cases.ts` before development measurements:
offset cadence, rising pulse and long breath. Report reused and new passages
separately. Confirm at both total allowances. Run the same comparison on facets
using the existing geometry recipe (`subdivisions: 0.5`), with no shape-specific
tuning against confirmation results.

Keep the original common accuracy ceilings and guide caps. Report best accuracy,
feasibility, actual work, unsuccessful attempts, and per-passage losses. Seed-block
bootstrap intervals characterize this small panel, not broad musical generality.
No invented aggregate headline combines accuracy and guide count. No algorithm
tuning follows inspection of confirmation data.

The gallery should expose matched forks, independent references and complete
alternatives for both shapes. The fork explanation must state the permissions
used for later sections, rather than imply that all later guides are always
allowed. Large tracks and observations stay local; compact evidence is committed.

## Candidate frozen before confirmation

Development measured 168 portfolios across seven variants. The previous
recommended search reproduced all 24 control portfolios exactly: track hashes,
RMS errors and physics work. Three/four active paths improved some intermediate
guide caps but reduced best accuracy. Preserving source permissions also caused
accuracy losses; this is retained as an explicit experiment, not enabled by default.

The preselected candidate is **`focused`**, implemented in clean compiler
**`fc3023a2`**. It retains the most accurate path and the intermediate path that
most improves coverage of the measured error envelope between its accurate and
sparse endpoints. When there is no useful intermediate, it follows the sparse
endpoint. All complete alternatives remain available for delivery. Two active
paths retain the previous per-branch allocation scale. Later guides remain
allowed, exactly as in the control; no guide quotas or music rules are added.

Across the 12 development case/seed pairs at 900,000 frames, best RMS is
0.029703 versus 0.029747, and half-guide-cap RMS is 0.043215 versus 0.049706.
At 450,000, those values are 0.034918 versus 0.034847 and 0.049699 versus
0.052834. Quarter-guide coverage and guide count at some common error ceilings
worsen. This is a targeted tradeoff improvement, not uniform superiority.

Freeze these choices before inspecting confirmation seeds 271–278. Compare
`candidate` (the previous balanced search) with `focused` on all nine passages,
both allowances and both shapes: **576 portfolios**. Separate the six reused
passages from the three new passages. Do not tune against these results.

A separate diagnostic probe uses exposed quick-pickups seed 242 and
staccato-release seed 243. It varies continuation allowance and later guide
permissions from the same physical prefixes. That work is outside the matched
portfolio allowance and makes no portfolio-efficiency claim. Its initial source
selection incorrectly assumed a half-cap winner would contain a non-startup
guide; the corrected probe also handles a zero-guide winner. The aborted log is
retained with the completed measurements.

## What the diagnosis established

In the previous candidate's 900,000-frame panel, 219 of 794 guide-forbidden forks
ended with **more** guided sections than their source. The fork prohibited one
guide but re-enabled later guides while rebuilding the suffix. This was permitted
behavior, not evidence that a guide was physically necessary. Of 100 eventual
intermediate cap winners that were available before later search rounds, 74 were never
selected as a source for further exploration. Availability alone does not prove
that exploring them would have helped; the controlled search variants test that.
The initial 112/138 diagnostic also counted candidates created before another
fork within the same round, when its source choices were already fixed. The
corrected count requires an actual later support boundary with another fork.

Broader exploration did help the middle, but its extra active paths reduced the
allowance per branch. At 900,000 frames, the three-path experiment's best RMS
worsened from 0.029747 to 0.032749 while half-cap RMS improved from 0.049706 to
0.042939. The focused two-path version recovered best accuracy while retaining
most of that intermediate improvement. This is why the final candidate changes
**which continuation receives the second path's work**, rather than adding paths.

The 48 completed continuation probes all passed timing and survival. They show
that more allowance alone is unreliable: 11 of 16 matched attempts have higher
target error at 250,000 frames than at 100,000. These are independent searches,
not extensions that retain the previous result. For example, a quick-pickups
continuation has RMS 0.029450 at 100,000 and 0.032756 at 250,000, with nine guided
sections in both. The portfolio retains all completed results, so later selection
can keep the better measured track.

Permission preservation has a different purpose. From the quick-pickups
zero-guide source, a 100,000-frame continuation with later guides allowed reaches
RMS 0.029295 using nine guides; preserving the zero-guide pattern reaches 0.100766
with none. From the staccato half-cap source, preservation can remove another
guide, but the accuracy change depends on the allowance. These experiments do
not establish a geometric ceiling. They show why preserving permissions belongs
in an explicit research option rather than a universal rule.

Repeated final geometry is another diagnostic, not automatically avoidable work.
The old and focused two-path searches each produced 11 repeated tracks in 426
development attempts at the larger allowance. Three-path coverage produced 24
in 560. A repeated result may only become known after searching; no blanket
claim that those physics frames could all be deleted is made.

## Fresh confirmation

All **576 portfolios** completed with a valid deliverable and a valid zero-guide
alternative. The panel contains **20,524 attempts**, of which **27 failed** the
physical/timing contract; all remain recorded. Each method paid for both starting
tracks, every fork, prefix verification and construction replay within its total
allowance. The baseline and focused search use the same frozen compiler build,
authored inputs, scorer and geometry recipe. Only the declared traversal differs.

The principal improvement is accuracy at intermediate guide limits. A half cap
means at most `floor(support sections / 2)` guided sections, applied identically
to both portfolios after search. All 72 passage/seed pairs are feasible in every
row below.

| Shape | Total allowance | Previous half-cap RMS | Focused half-cap RMS | Reduction | Seed-block 95% interval for RMS difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Arcs | 450,000 | 0.056285 | 0.050488 | 10.30% | −0.009024 to −0.002758 |
| Arcs | 900,000 | 0.056175 | 0.046992 | 16.35% | −0.013215 to −0.005112 |
| Facets | 450,000 | 0.056706 | 0.050880 | 10.27% | −0.009736 to −0.002300 |
| Facets | 900,000 | 0.047825 | 0.043817 | 8.38% | −0.005856 to −0.002352 |

At 900,000 frames, arcs improve 41 pairs, worsen nine and tie 22 at that cap;
facets improve 32, worsen 21 and tie 19. The largest gains come from quick pickups:
their eight-seed mean falls from 0.125714 to 0.071875 for arcs, and 0.087290 to
0.057759 for facets. Every arc passage improves its mean at this cap, but the
facet improvement is less broadly distributed.

Best unrestricted accuracy remains nearly unchanged. Research adherence of the
lowest-RMS selections changes from **869.165 to 869.943** for arcs and
**871.452 to 871.606** for facets at 900,000 frames. The seed-block intervals for
the best-RMS differences include zero. These are research-panel means, not V4
headlines, and do not establish a new general accuracy breakthrough.

Actual work is also nearly unchanged. At 900,000, mean construction frames are
888,281 → 888,348 for arcs and 887,797 → 888,098 for facets; changes across all
four shape/budget comparisons are below 0.04%. This rules out substantially more
realized physics work as the explanation for the half-cap gain. Wall times on
the shared host are recorded but do not support a controlled speedup claim.

### New passages are a separate check

The three newly authored passages contribute 24 pairs per shape and allowance.
They were not used for development. At 900,000 frames:

| Shape | Previous half-cap RMS | Focused half-cap RMS | Seed-block 95% interval for difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Arcs | 0.054201 | 0.049821 | −0.008705 to +0.000090 |
| Facets | 0.047741 | 0.047756 | −0.001425 to +0.001390 |

The arc mean improves, with uncertainty extending just across zero. Facets show
essentially no effect on these three passages. At 450,000, the new-passage means
are 0.055795 → 0.055867 for arcs and 0.054088 → 0.052721 for facets, again with
intervals spanning zero. Transfer to facets works operationally and improves the
full panel's middle choices; these data do **not** establish broad transfer to
unseen music. Eight seeds and three new authored programs remain a small sample.

### Losses and recommendation

The focused search is not uniformly better across the tradeoff. At 450,000, arc
quarter-cap RMS worsens from 0.065786 to 0.067298; its delta interval is entirely
positive. At 900,000, arc three-quarter-cap RMS worsens from 0.037685 to 0.038770.
Facets have quarter-cap losses too, especially on the reused passages. At the
common baseline-best +0.020 error ceiling, mean guide count increases from
2.569 to 2.653 for arcs and 1.903 to 1.972 for facets at 900,000.

Keep **focused coverage as an explicit research option** for improving the
intermediate range. The existing balanced option and its measurements remain
available. Do not replace production settings or claim that one search dominates
every artistic preference. All algorithms were frozen before confirmation; the
fresh-seed losses were not followed by tuning against the same panel.
