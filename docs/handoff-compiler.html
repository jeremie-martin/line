01How the compiler works

The hand-off optimizer (compileHandoff, scripts/v0/optimizer/handoff.ts) is the active compiler. Its core idea: a search state is a partial track prefix at a gap boundary, not a whole re-generated track. The search extends one gap at a time, keeps alternatives on a deterministic stack, and is bounded by a hard sim-frame budget.

The mental model

A spec is a sequence of contacts (the rider must touch a line at a beat) separated by gaps. Each section carries axis targets — speed, air, grain, contact-style. Compiling means choosing, for each gap, a small arc of lines (a "catch") that (a) lands the rider on the next contact within ±1 frame, (b) keeps it alive, and (c) hits the section's axis targets as closely as possible. The hand-off search builds that chain of catches greedily-but-with-backtracking, always holding onto the best complete-or-partial track it has seen.

The search loop

1

Resolve start options

Before the first gap, the search builds up to 10 candidate start states (position + velocity), ranked by a two-gap feasibility probe. The start is a one-time choice, so exploring several start subtrees is cheap relative to the per-gap search.

buildStartOptions() · START_OPTION_LIMIT = 10

2

Pop a prefix from the frontier

Two stacks form the frontier: a pass stack (skip-free prefixes) and a fallback stack (prefixes that had to skip a contact). The pass stack is always drained first, so the search prioritises prefixes that have landed every contact so far.

activeFrontier() · passStack / fallbackStack

3

Score the prefix & register it

Every popped prefix — complete or partial — is detected, turned into a drift report, and offered to a strict best-so-far register. Partial prefixes are scored on the sections they actually completed, so a track that dies early still counts for what it got right.

evaluateNode() → BestSoFarRegister.consider()

4

Generate & rank the next gap's candidates

For the current gap, sample 16 arcs, keep a pool of 5–8 sorted by cost, then re-rank them by a feasibility probe plus penalties (below). Add two special candidate sources — catch-reuse and brake catches — then branch on the best 3.

rankedOptions() · HANDOFF_N_CAND 16 · HANDOFF_BRANCHING 3

5

Push children, repeat until budget

The 3 children go back on the appropriate stack. The loop runs until the frontier empties or the sim-frame budget is hit. A hard limit of budget × 1.2 throws PhysicsFrameLimitExceeded as a backstop. The register's best entry is the returned track.

getSimFrames() ≥ budgetUnits → break

Candidate ranking — the heart of it

For each candidate the search computes a score (lower is better) in scoreCandidateForHandoff:

score = candidate.cost   + scarcity        // dead-end probe: is the NEXT contact still reachable?   + statePenalty   // punish wild exit velocity / steep angles   + overshoot     // asymmetric speed & air OVER-target penalty

Feasibility probe

Before committing a catch, the search simulates one gap into the future (previewFutureContacts, horizon 1) and asks: does the next contact still have surviving candidates? Zero survivors → a heavy DEAD_END_PENALTY; few survivors → a smaller scarcity cost. This is what stops the greedy chain from walking into a corner.

Overshoot penalty

Catch arcs are net-downhill, so the rider tends to accelerate past target speed over a long track and eventually stall. An asymmetric penalty (weight 16 on speed, 16 on air) prefers catches whose achieved speed/air does not exceed target — pure selection, no geometry change, so it never destabilises the candidate generator.

Two special candidate sources

♻️ Catch-reuse

On a periodic rhythm the rider reaches a near-steady state, so a catch that landed three contacts ago lands this one too. Catch arcs are sled-relative, so reuseCatchCandidates translates the last 2 committed catches by the sled delta and offers the survivors — found in ~1 simulation instead of re-rolling 16 random arcs. This is what lets a budget-starved periodic spec finish within budget.

🛑 Brake catches

When the rider runs over a moderate target speed, brakeCatchCandidates offers uphill-entry arcs (negative start angle) that bleed speed on the way up before contacting. They're decoupled from landing (the impact anchor still places them on the contact), so on non-creeping specs they simply lose the ranking — no collateral damage.

The two hard rules

Determinism: same spec + seed → byte-identical track, every time. Every randomized step uses a seed derived from the gap index. Budget-monotonicity: a larger budget can only ever produce an equal-or-better result. Both are enforced by tests/optimizer_handoff.test.ts and were never allowed to break — every mechanism below only adds candidates or re-ranks them; none mutate committed geometry.

Phase 2 · Exploit periodicity 140 → 188

The breakthrough that broke the first "ceiling." Periodic specs reach a steady state where one catch shape works repeatedly — but per-gap RNG re-rolled fresh arcs every gap and never reused the known-good one.

c9801a0Add catch-reuse to handoff search (exploit periodicity)140 → 188›

On uniform-rhythm specs the rider settles into a near-steady state where the same sled-relative catch lands contact after contact. The search was blind to this, re-rolling 16 random arcs and backtracking at every gap.

Catch arcs now record the sled reference they were placed from (GapFit.ref, set in sampleOneCandidate). reuseCatchCandidates translates the last 2 committed catches by the sled delta and offers any survivors as extra candidates — 1 sim each. Deterministic and monotonic (only adds candidates). opening_burst 10→227, drums_signature 59→431.

Phase 3 · Speed-creep control 188 → 315

The systemic root cause of the remaining cap: speed creep. Net-downhill catches make the rider accelerate to ~2× target and stall before the last contacts. Fixed in two ways — penalise overshoot in selection, then generate braking catches.

c49b19fPenalize speed-overshoot in candidate ranking→ 192›

Candidate cost penalised speed error symmetrically (1 of 4 axes), far too weakly to arrest accumulating creep.

Added an asymmetric term that only punishes achieved-speed above target. Selection-only — it picks slower catches that already exist, never changing geometry.

28dd652Tune speed-overshoot weight 8→16→ 195›

The weight landscape is non-monotonic (search interactions are chaotic), so each point was measured rather than assumed. 16 beat 8 suite-wide.

8ab8594Add air-overshoot penalty to ranking→ 200›

Air-time is the other systematic suite-wide over-target axis (the rider hangs longer than asked). Same asymmetric selection-only bias, weight 16. Lifted axis quality on cold_start, drums_signature, grain, tiny_dance.

667bf16Add uphill-entry brake candidates (decoupled speed control)→ ~300›

Selection can only pick a slow catch, never create one — so on genuine creep specs (solo_run) there was nothing slow to pick. This commit generates braking catches.

A brake flag samples an uphill (negative, −28°…−6°) start angle so the rider climbs the arc's front and bleeds speed before contacting. Crucially decoupled from landing — the impact anchor still lands the contact — so brakes are offered as extra candidates and only win when the overshoot penalty rewards their lower speed. Earlier attempts that braked via the exit angle or by shortening arcs broke landing on legitimately-fast specs; this decoupling is what made it net-positive.

6021116Brake from first overshoot (ratio 1.1→1.0)↑›

Creep can't be recovered late — once the rider is well over target it's too few gaps from the end to bleed it off. Lowering the trigger ratio to 1.0 brakes at the earliest sign of overshoot, pre-empting creep instead of chasing it.

7bdea1cTune brake candidate count K 2→3↑›

A richer brake pool (3 attempts per gap) recovered more specs — but only where the budget could afford it (next commit).

b94b8adScale brake-K inversely with contact density→ 315›

A deep run like solo_run (77 contacts) is budget-starved — spending 3 brake sims at every gap starves its completion. A short spec can afford the richer pool.

brakeK = clamp(round(165 / contacts), 2, 3) — a compute-per-contact allocation, not a spec-name gate. Deep specs get K=2 (room to finish), short specs K=3 (the pool that recovers drums_pendulum). solo_run 0→55 (2/3 seeds), pass rate 0.69→0.82.

04What we learned

~38 mechanisms were tried; the pattern that emerged is sharp and reusable. The candidate generator is delicately tuned, and the search dynamics are chaotic — so the levers that pay off are the ones that don't touch generated geometry.

✅ Levers that worked

  • Selection re-ranking — overshoot penalties (speed, air) pick better catches with zero geometry change. clean
  • Reuse — translate known-good catches on periodic specs. ~1 sim
  • Decoupled braking — generate uphill catches that lose harmlessly when not needed. no collateral
  • One-time costs — start-option expansion doesn't starve the DFS. bounded
  • Throughput — fewer candidates / cheaper previews → more search per frame. efficiency
  • Density-aware allocation — spend brake budget where the spec can afford it. per-contact

❌ Dead ends (reverted)

  • Per-gap escalation — 48-sample re-roll lands hard contacts but fires too broadly and blows budget. 74–85
  • Adaptive N_CAND — starvation doesn't track contact count. 101
  • Exit-angle / length braking — breaks landing on fast specs. chaotic
  • Grain-bias sampling — changing geometry regresses hard. 251
  • Completion repair pass — steals budget from the search. 26
  • Tail-completion ungating — greedy multi-gap drive starves syncopated/verse. 234

The one-line takeaway

Selection-based and one-time levers compound; generation-based knobs (escalation, sampling, candidate counts, grain bias) are chaotic and net-negative. The candidate generator is a tuned instrument — re-rank what it produces, reuse what already worked, and add escape-hatch candidates that lose gracefully, but don't reach in and change the arcs it draws.

05The 40k ceiling

354.55 is a verified local optimum at the fixed --budget=40000, reached after exhausting the lever space above.

The budget is 100% saturated — every spec/seed row spends ≥ 40,000 sim-frames. That makes the remaining gap a hard trade-off rather than a tuning problem: any added mechanism starves a spec, removing work loses quality, and the per-candidate physics-validation cost is irreducible.

The three still-failing rows (solo_run s0, opening_burst s0/s2) are budget-bound, not idea-bound — they already pass at ~80–120k. Reaching the original >600 target therefore needs either a higher campaign budget or a research-grade rebuild of search thoroughness and axis quality. All progress, mechanisms, and dead-ends are recorded for whenever that budget or architecture decision is made.
