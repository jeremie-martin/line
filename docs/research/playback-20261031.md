The loop metric measures media seek completion, **not** a guarantee of
continuous screen presentation. Native loops can still have a visible seam,
and changing A/B soundtrack intentionally reloads at the current position.
Missed video callbacks are not counted as dropped frames. Firefox's native
loop `mediaTime` advances across loops, and its `presentedFrames` counter in
these headless samples is not consistently comparable to totalVideoFrames;
neither is used to claim display smoothness. Earlier scratch calculations of
loop gaps from expected-display timestamps were discarded: timestamps can
move backwards around seeks. Physical scanout/perceived smoothness remains
outside these browser measurements.

Implementation: one `<video loop>`, no follower and no running rate corrections.
`pair_media.ts` uses ffmpeg/ffprobe to cache a combined picture in desktop or
stacked layout, remuxed with either original AAC track. Only a new pair,
layout or soundtrack loads media. Pause/position survive layout changes;
newer requests cancel pending loads. Original clips, manifests and judgments
are not rewritten. Cache identity includes source bytes and encoding recipe.
There is no extra player framework or diagnostic code on ordinary page loads.

Media checks cover all 108 frames of a real pair in both layouts: same frame
count/order and identical picture packets between soundtrack choices; audio
packet hashes and timestamps exactly match each original. Combining pictures
requires re-encoding (CRF 12), so pixels are not identical: SSIM across the
four compared crops is 0.999339–0.999703. This is a derived preview, not a new
published study artifact. Unit/integration tests cover layouts, source
preservation, concurrent caching, duration validation and manifest-only API
lookup. Chromium and Firefox UI checks cover sound, speed, pause, scrubbing,
mobile layout, navigation with mocked saves, and rapid soundtrack changes.
The integrated page also completed six fresh-load captures per headless browser
(Chromium and Firefox; two pairs × three sound choices), with no page errors,
speed corrections or reported dropped frames. All 308 tests, typecheck and
reachability checks pass.

The expanded scripts, baseline snapshot, prototypes and raw measurements stay
local under `generated/pair-playback-experiment/`; they are not a new committed
benchmark workflow. The earlier production measurements below still apply;
this change addresses pair synchronization, not an unproven production stall.

## Original investigation

Question: playback in `motion-gallery/production.html` and `pairs.html` was
sometimes laggy, and Trouble impacts sometimes looked a few milliseconds off the
music. What caused it, and what is the simplest design under which it cannot happen?
The same player then went into the spec dashboard and the grading page (second
part, below).

Answer:
- The lag came from the pages' clocks, not from drawing or the network.
- The playback core was **rewritten, not patched**: `shared/playback.js`.
- About 15 ms of the "off" timing is deliberate in the compiler (the felt-jolt offset).

Evidence: `playback-20261031.json`, which holds every before/after measurement
below. Method: Playwright, headless Chromium 1223 and Firefox 150. The ride is
`trouble-variants-v2/kick-101` from 14 s; the study is
`night-pairs-2026-10-05`, pair 1. Each condition runs for 10 s. The page is
reached through an insecure host name (as over the VPN). CPU and network
throttling go through CDP (Chromium only). The page and the harness each read
`requestVideoFrameCallback` and the audio context independently.

## Causes

1. **Production, Firefox: the ride stuttered.** The page drew the ride at
   `audio.currentTime`. Firefox updates that value about every 40 ms, so the
   drawn time repeated on 354 of about 600 frames, then jumped (median step
   error 17 ms). Chromium's `currentTime` is smooth (0.07 ms).
2. **Production, both browsers: about one frame late.**
   - A canvas frame drawn in `requestAnimationFrame` reaches the screen a
     refresh after its timestamp.
   - Drawing the position at the time of reading therefore showed each pose
     about 17 ms late at 60 Hz.
   - This assumes `currentTime` is the audible position, as browsers intend.
3. **Pair study with sound: seek storms.**
   - The page re-seeked clip B whenever it differed from A by more than 40 ms.
   - A sounding clip runs on the audio clock and restarts late after every seek
     or unmute, so the gap reappeared at once.
   - Results with sound on B: 178 seeks of B in 10 s, 180 stalls, and B's own
     sound re-seeked about 18 times a second. At the hit, the two clips' frames
     were 2 or more apart.
   - In Firefox this happened even with sound off (33 seeks), because its
     coarse `currentTime` readings differ by more than 40 ms anyway.
4. **Not the cause:**
   - **Drawing:** 1.1 to 1.7 ms of script per frame unthrottled. It reaches
     7.5 ms only at 4x CPU slowdown with a 2x pixel ratio, where the page falls
     to 30 fps. The vendored renderer strokes all 4,910 lines every frame,
     which costs about 0.6 ms at 4x.
   - **Network:** opening a ride fetches 3.8 MB (manifest 366 KB, ride record
     1.98 MB, MP3 1.1 MB). Every response is `Cache-Control: no-store`, so this
     repeats on every open. Over plain HTTP the SHA-256 checks run in
     JavaScript. Opening takes 0.6 s locally and 3.3 s at 4x CPU slowdown,
     40 ms RTT and 20 Mbit/s. After opening, playback uses no network.

to 40 ms more in Firefox.

## Design

One module, `shared/playback.js` (beside `shared/theme.css`, which both apps
already load), used by every page that plays music.

**Production page.**
- The verified MP3 bytes are decoded once and played with Web Audio.
- `AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()` pairs the context time being heard with
  `performance.now()`, output latency included.
- Each animation frame draws the ride at the position heard at its display time
  (the frame timestamp plus one refresh). Nothing is estimated from `currentTime`.
- Firefox stamps the start of the audio block being played (blocks of about 11
  to 21 ms here) with the moment of reading. The clock therefore takes the
  freshest pairing of the last second; the context clock never jumps.
- The `<audio>` element and its blob URL are gone.

**Pair page.**
- The sounding clip, or A when muted, leads and keeps the browser's own
  audio/video sync.
- The other clip compares the frames both presented
  (`requestVideoFrameCallback`) and adjusts by playback rate only:
  - waits at rate 0 (held, and resumed on the next frame; pause/play took 26 to
    39 ms to show a new frame);
  - catches up at +5%;
  - acts only on a gap of more than 1.5 frames, then continues until level;
  - seeks only when more than 0.25 s apart.
- Nothing seeks a playing clip except the existing loop restart.
- Unchanged: clip content, the light, the timeline, choices, prompt, labels and
  manifest handling.

Tests: `tests/playback.test.ts` covers the pure pieces:
- the heard position;
- the output clock under Firefox-style block stamps;
- the display time;
- the last frame;
- the on-screen gap, including a first frame that waits for its sound;
- the follower rule.

## Second part: the spec dashboard and the legacy pages

**Spec dashboard** (`spec-dashboard/`), where specs are authored and notes
placed by ear.
- Before, the cursor came from an `<audio>` element's `currentTime`. Where the
  spec ran past the music, a separate wall-clock "simulated" cursor took over,
  switching back when the music resumed. Landing clicks were scheduled on a
  second `AudioContext` from the element's time.
- After, the music is decoded and played by `createMusic`, and the cursor is
  drawn at the spec time heard when the frame is on screen.
- The audio context's clock runs whether or not a sample plays. So a cursor
  before the music (a positive music offset), after it, or with no music linked
  runs on the same clock, and the simulated mode is gone.
- Clicks are scheduled on the same context at the exact context time of each
  landing in the music (`music.when`). They now also sound where the spec runs
  past the music.
- Pausing stores the position heard at the moment of the key press. A note or
  moment placed after pausing gets that time; a moment anchor was checked to
  equal the paused, heard time.
- Unchanged: the music offset, seeking, ±1 s / ±5 s, the end-of-spec stop,
  Reload keeping the cursor, and the note and spec formats. The debug "agent
  context" pane reports `clock: "audio"` or `"paused"`; the `"spec"` value of
  the removed mode is gone.

## Not verified

  especially). `getOutputTimestamp` is meant to include it; headless audio is a
  fake sink.
- Display latency beyond the next refresh (compositor, monitor). The page
  assumes the next refresh.
- Firefox and Safari on real hardware. Safari was not tested at all. Also
  whether Firefox and Safari trim this MP3's encoder delay (a Lavc-written
  LAME header): only Chromium was checked; untrimmed it would be +21.7 ms.
- How the 60 fps clip renders map video time to the 40 fps physics frames.
- `scripts/gallery/check_production_ui.mjs`: its music assertions now read the
  play button. It was already stale before this change (port 8767, a removed
  comparison link) and was not run.
