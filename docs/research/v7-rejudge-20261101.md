Each run: `npm run compare -- run --reference=main --candidate=<branch> --panel=v7`
(4 rounds, 22 automatic specs plus 6 explicit). Hypotheses are stated before
the runs.

## Hypotheses
- **R0, delete the ending refinement** (`exp/no-tail-refinement`). Last
  night: −0.2 on bench, one sealed long ride lost 66 points.
  - Hypothesis: the V7 headline moves by less than 1 point, with about 5%
    less compute, and any loss concentrates on P6 (the long ride: its ending).
- **S0, no steep-arrival preference at all** (`exp/no-steep-arrival`: the
  `steepArrivalFrom` setting removed). Last night only "everywhere" was
  tested (−12.5). This is the owner's question: is the mechanism needed at
  all, not where its threshold sits?
  - No directional hypothesis: the October adoption suggests a loss on strong
    asks (P3, P4, P6), but the compiler has changed since. Every ruler is
    reported.

## A smooth steep-arrival preference (hypotheses stated before the runs)
- **Structural step** (`exp/arrival-weight`): the threshold drives one
  weight (0 or 1) that scales four things:
  - the arrival-angle preference;
  - the arrival-speed target (adding the impulse);
  - the upright-arrival preference;
  - the ordinary heading preference, in reverse.

  Byte-identical: compile parity PASS.
- **L-smooth** (`exp/arrival-smooth`): the weight rises smoothly from 0 at
  ask 0.4 to 1 at 0.8 (0.5 at the old threshold). It removes the cliff.
  - Hypothesis: within ±2 of `main` on the V7 headline, with P3 and P5
    (which gained without the preference) not worse.
- **L-linear** (`exp/arrival-linear`): the weight is the ask itself, so there
  is no threshold and no parameter.
  - No directional hypothesis: it applies some of the preference to light
    asks, where the full preference everywhere lost 12.5.
