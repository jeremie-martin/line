# Taste and the compiler

Notes from November 14, 2026, after the rails study and the owner's feedback. They record what happened, the owner's
concerns, and what to be mindful of. They are not a plan: what the best solution is remains open.

## What happened

- During the compiler brief, changes were adopted because the V8 headline rose: guides on strong phrases, families
  placed where they fit, the refitted table, guides where they clearly fit (+20 in all). Nothing measured what the owner
  cares about. Rails went from 54% to 70% of gaps, and clean upper hits on strong beats from 9.6% to 7.1%. It showed only
  once ride character was measured (`tools/measure/character.ts`, the "ride character" line in every comparison).
- The first try at undoing it used the same lever, the plan. Two kinds of change were tried:
  - strong phrases back to open arcs: −5.4;
  - a rail budget: −12.5 to −87.
  Both cut rails mainly by making more of the ride plain arcs. The budget also built shapes without a rail, which leave
  the rider slow. Nothing was adopted (REWORK.md, November 14).
- The owner's verdict on those changes:
  - plain open arcs on strong phrases hurt variety;
  - a budget is the wrong idea: it may appear in a comparison, but not as a solution;
  - restricting only the arcs is an odd rule.

## The owner's concerns

- **The search will make the ride boring if it decides everything.** Left to pick the best candidate, it uses rails
  everywhere (our best tool for the score) or one geometry everywhere. A version once scored 950 with boring tracks.
- **What we want is hard to define,** and may not be one objective number. Even with a number, how to use it (a price in
  the search, a rule, a comparison) is an open question.
- **The plan is not a ruling.** Pre-planning the families is a cheap way to get variety, and it has worked well. An
  earlier version kept one geometry for three gaps in a row. It was crude and visible, yet gave varied, fun rides. A
  simple mechanism can be enough.
- **Upper hits.** The owner loves them: the rider is thrown off the end of some geometry, flies, and is caught by a line
  above. Such a catch could do a rail's job (it drives the rider down and can add speed) and may score well too.
- **Variety of families.** Today's mix is fine with him. Whether the realized shapes look alike is a separate, later
  question.

## How the work went, and what to do differently

- **Taste was not measured, so it lost.** Measure what the owner cares about first, then read every change through it:
  the geometry sheets and the ride character line, beside the headline.
- **Two options were offered as if they were the only ones.** Lay out the levels a problem can be worked at, and say
  what each option does to variety, not only to the number that prompted it.
- **The budget was chosen because it was simple,** though the owner had named a price. Keep track of the ideas he
  raises.
- **"More open arcs" was reported as good.** Fewer rails is not more variety: more open arcs can also mean more of the
  same.
- **Too many quick iterations.** Prefer one well-understood, discussed step to several fast ones.

## Guiding principles

None of these is a decision.

- The score is an instrument. A change that alters the look is shown by its look before it is adopted.
- The kind of geometry is never left to the best candidate alone. Some logic outside the score keeps the ride varied.
- Mechanisms stay simple, clean and on purpose. Crude but clear beats clever but opaque.
- No quotas or budgets as the answer.
- An experiment is one implementation of an idea. A loss does not dismiss the idea.
- A rail is a tool the search may use, not the default. Today's shapes depend on theirs.

## Directions on the table

These work at different levels, and none has been chosen.

- **Measurement:**
  - the blind upper-hit study (`labels/studies/upper-hits-2026-11`): is the strength measure fair to upper hits?
  - ride character in every report.
- **Constructions that make the owner's preferences competitive:**
  - a receiver for upper hits;
  - shapes that keep their speed without a rail.
  If everything were equally competitive, the tension would go away. That is unlikely, but there is room to improve.
- **Planning and search together,** kept simple. Families placed where they fit (a fitted table, kept in the
  foundation) is one such approach, not a settled design: any of these may replace it. Some possibilities:
  - draw two families for a run of gaps, search both, keep the better;
  - give the candidate pool a mix of families, mostly the drawn one and some of the others, with the mix free to vary;
  - keep one geometry over several gaps.
- **Prices in the search:** a cost for a kept rail, or for repeating the last family. This adapts to the ride. Where it
  goes and how to keep it from being gamed are open questions.

## Facts worth keeping

All on V8, 196 rides, unless stated.

- **The strong-phrase guide rule** did most of the rails' rise, in one step. It is also the step where clean upper hits
  fell most.
- **Where clean upper hits come from:** only arcs and scattered. Open arcs catch 29% of their strong beats with one;
  shaped families never do (0 of about 4,000). Within arcs and scattered, gaps keeping a rail catch 7–11% clean, those
  without 22–33%. (A beat is caught by the lines of the gap that starts at it.)
- **Clean upper hits by the strike measure:** the softest catch on strong beats (bias −0.18 against −0.09 for floor
  catches at the same asks), and head-down twice as often.
- **Shapes built without a rail:** speed error about 5× that of any other gap; the rider is too slow.
- **Distinct families per ride** did not move through any of this. It does not see rails or upper hits.
