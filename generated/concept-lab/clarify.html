A few things to clarify first

Before rendering a whole new round, here is how I read the points that were unclear, conflicting, or that I may have got wrong. Most need one tap. Then the list you asked about: what is settled for a first version, and what isn’t.

One change first: fresh rides

All seven lab songs are recompiled with today’s compiler, with the same songs and seeds. All are complete and valid, and they’re very different tracks: Los Voltaje went from 5,911 lines to 7,804. The next films will use them. The old rides stay in round 5.

One consequence already: the new tracks have many more steep rides. The “steep rides drop” camera I built for Los Voltaje’s dive would fire 5 to 10 times per song now, not just a few times. That is part of question 4.

v1: settled, open, later

You asked whether we’re a few iterations away. I think yes, about two: this round (strong hits, camera, colours), then one on the fresh rides with a real phone and YouTube check. Then v1 can be ported to production. Here is how I’d file everything:

Settled for v1

  • Looks: Paper, Hokusai and Black, the three you’d post. Dune and Plum too. Lagoon needs a re-watch without the afterimage.
  • Camera: Tamed placement, 15 % closer, a steady zoom by default. The opening and ending show the whole course, and the band is there from frame 0.
  • Line: pencil → ink, inked as the rider passes. Whole rail and All ink are the alternatives.
  • Hits: Held, driven by the hit’s real strength, never a rank or a top-N.
  • Band: bars or fork, low-middle, with red on the loudest bars (or Heat).
  • Background: plain or Whisper. Night is the hand-placed drop cue. The intensity metric drives only light things.
  • Title: the song’s name, in the line’s colour, at most two lines, behind the line.

Open: this round and next

  • The “oh yeah” on the biggest hits.
  • Rider height, and whether a steep ride should drop.
  • Growing the colours to 10–20: which candidates join.
  • Zoom beyond steady, and who chooses it.
  • Everything re-checked on the fresh rides.
  • A real delivery check: a phone, sound, YouTube’s compression.

Later: production

  • How a song gets its look: hand hints, favourite weights, cohesion. Also a colour change at the drop.
  • A small per-song hint sheet: the Night cue, maybe zoom, maybe tone.
  • The port to the production renderer.
  • The compiler’s timing jolt (the other agent’s track).

Parked, not dead

  • Afterimage, the ending ring, the larger rider, Ridge, Score, hits by source, the vignette.
  • Iris and Three colours (question 9).
