What changed since the older productions?

The June–September stack first rendered the ride with a following camera and authored zoom. Strong authored beats could add a zoom punch. A second compositor then applied a 10 px two-axis vibration, up to 0.4° rotation, and constant 4% overscan. Its vibration accumulated recent impacts and used absolute-time sine waves.

Today the single canvas renderer follows the ten-point body centre, places the rider according to travel direction, and uses steady zoom. The shake is applied to the ride only: it starts at a measured force peak, points away from the struck surface, and oscillates at 6 Hz. At strength 0.85 it starts with a 28 px jump. The split has a separate small overscan, even with movement disabled.

“Old vibration” recovers the old motion formula using today’s measured peaks. It deliberately keeps the current colour design and camera. The archived video preserves the actual September appearance, including its old ruler, geometry, timing, zoom and layout; it is not a fair A/B for motion alone.

Ride provenance and comparison conditions
Draws through scripts/video/picture.js, with the current theme registry, split, colour response, inking and music band. One shared audio clock; effects and cameras are pure functions of time. The same functions draw these curves and pictures. Dense sequences accumulate bounded candidate motion; the current response chooses one winning hit. No score is being optimized.
