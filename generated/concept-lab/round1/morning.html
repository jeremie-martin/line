A fresh look, from the ride outward

The earlier rounds re-skinned one composition: a small rider above a bar chart. This round steps back and changes the bigger things instead: how large the ride is, what fills the lower half, what a hit does, how the camera follows the song, and how a video opens and ends. Everything is drawn from the real saved rides and music.

What I learned along the way

  • Speed decides everything. Framed closer, the rider crosses about three screen widths per second. Anything left behind in the world (marks, trails, ghosts) is gone almost instantly, so the music and the “memory” belong in the frame itself, in the lower band.
  • Half the track is scaffolding. Only about half the lines are ever touched. Drawn in pencil, they recede, and black ink comes to mean “the music touched here”.
  • Four hits in five are drums. So drums stay black, and colour becomes an event: voice in blue, other instruments in vermilion.
  • Speed hardly changes between sections, but density does. The “Sections” camera therefore follows how many hits a section has: wider when sparse, closer when dense.
  • Accents fire at the touch. The drawn rider meets the line 25–75 ms before the measured strike time, so every accent uses the moment of contact.

Inside the lab, “Today” approximates the shipped shake, colour split and flash; the films use the real shipped video. Everything was checked muted, at phone size in a browser, not on your phone.
