Wada’s Dictionary of Color Combinations

You suggested using what designers already use rather than inventing colours. The classic is Sanzo Wada’s Dictionary of Color Combinations (1933): 348 hand-made combinations of two to four colours, still a reference today and freely available as data. Every new palette comes from one of them, numbered so you can look it up.

The work was giving each colour a role (paper, line, bars, accent), and your verdicts gave the rules:

  • the bars carry more colour than the paper;
  • paper and bars stay in one family;
  • a dark paper can be near-neutral, but a mid-tone one can’t;
  • warm accents over cool or neutral bars.

From 228 combinations of three or four colours, 103 fitted the rules and 40 were rendered. These twelve were kept by eye.

Two pairs overlap: Viridian and Indigo Teal, and Magenta and Old Rose. One of each may be enough. Gilt’s accent is darker than its bars, so it reads a little less clearly.

The other two

  • Kraft: your darker paper against the lighter one with a brick-red accent, as you suggested.
  • Your hits, together: you chose each part on its own, so this checks the combination against today’s full set, on a light and a dark palette.
