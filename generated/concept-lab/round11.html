Round eleven · 7 November

Stronger hits, and more Wada

Four questions in the lab: two on the hits, two on new palettes from Wada’s dictionary.

→Four questionsThe split over the band, how to make the hits stronger, then light and dark palettes.

What drives the hits

What the ride actually did. Every effect uses each impact’s measured strength and measured moment, never the spec’s target. On Los Voltaje, Petrunko and Tomada every planned contact happened. The ride also makes 21–31 extra, unplanned touches a song, all very weak (strength 0.16 at most, where the hardest is 0.85), so nothing shows for them.

Why your set felt weaker

Today’s reaches almost full force from strength 0.45, so a typical hit already shakes fully. Ours grows steeply with strength, so a median hit gets about half of today’s split:

The split scales the same way. The flash is unchanged: toward the paper, from 0.80.

The split over the band: it is now the default, as you prefer. The title and the sun always split, since they sit behind the ride. Question 1 checks the band.

More Wada

You asked for light papers other than cream. Wada’s own light colours are mostly creams and yellows, so the new papers come from each combination’s own blues, greens and roses, made pale: ice blue, periwinkle, celadon, blush, lilac and cool grey. What made it work: the paper must differ in hue from the bars, warm bars on ice blue or celadon, cool bars on blush. A pale paper in the bars’ own hue looked weak.

There are also six darks, two drop partners taken from their light schemes’ own combinations, and Brick’s accent, deepened so it no longer melts into the paper. Each palette names its Wada combination. Etruscan, Peony and Umber share the same ice-blue paper.
