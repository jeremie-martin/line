What the official camera really does

You remembered it well. It is a soft dead zone covering about 40 % of the frame: the camera barely moves while the rider is inside, and the rider pushes it along at the edge. So a fast rider sits right of centre, and lower when falling. Inside the zone you see the rider’s true motion, which is probably why it feels natural.

  • The rider’s width position hardly moves: 60–62 % of the frame, because speed is nearly the same everywhere (median 360–430 units/s across 22 rides). So speed can’t drive much on its own. What moves is the height.
  • You were right about Operator. The rider climbed the screen in straight ramps, then dropped: a sawtooth. My slip count couldn’t see that, because it counts events, not shape. A return now curves and settles, which alone cut Tamed’s slips by about two thirds.

What changed

  • New cameras: the rider sits right of centre. Moving with its real motion is free, so a fall looks like a fall, and the return is gentle. The looks use Calm, the stillest moving option; question 1 checks that choice.
  • Zoom is a slow tide, or nothing. Impact rate only stops pumping at about 3 seconds of smoothing, which is why last round’s zooms felt too fast. Tides now move at most 4 % a second and never while airborne.
  • Sections no longer drive anything. Tones follow a continuous signal, and you can compare the two: impact rate and loudness agree only partly (correlation 0.69).
  • Opening and ending work with any camera. The view blends between the overview and the camera in a way that keeps the rider in frame by construction. The path line is gone.
  • New to try: strength as duration (“Held”), a red that rests until a clearly stronger hit earns it (“Standing”), the Ridge (the course as a mountain, inking as you ride), and the Shade (a deliberate risk).
More detail
  • Night needs a human. Two automatic drop detectors failed across 22 rides, and both missed Petrunko’s real drop. So Night here is one cue per song, which I picked by hand from the spec’s own structure. That uses sections as an authoring aid, as you described. All six songs have one here to compare, but Petrunko’s, for example, has no rise in loudness, so whether a song deserves Night is a judgement call.
  • Why strong hits don’t stand out enough: the kick was only 1.27× larger on a strong hit than a median one, and half the hits lost about 15 % of their peak to frame timing. “Held” fixes both, and nothing runs past 0.25 s.
  • Gone: ink pressure, the zoom pulse, letterbox bars, pre-ink, section colours, the mirrored fork, drop-outs, locked-off shots and the director zooms.
  • Remotion: noted. This canvas could become the renderer itself; we can decide that once the look settles.

How it was made

Two agents helped: one proposed the band, red and hit ideas, one reviewed everything at phone size before I rendered any films, and caught several problems. Their notes are in generated/concept-lab/agents/.

Caveats: nothing has been checked on your phone or with sound on. Curves and counts are guardrails, not proof: the motion judgement is yours. Production is unchanged and nothing is committed.
