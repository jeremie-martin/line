Vibrance, exactly

Vibrance won rounds 33 and 34 on every theme. This page says exactly what it does, with its curves, and the round takes its three settings apart so we can find the right mix.

What vibrance does

Every theme is a handful of colours: the paper, the track, the bars, the sun, the hot note. Vibrance changes only how colourful each one is (its chroma, in OKLCH: how far from grey), never its hue or lightness, and gives most to the colours that have least:

new chroma = chroma × (1 + strength × amount × (1 − chroma / 0.2))

  • Chroma: 0 is grey; a paper is about 0.03, bars about 0.10, a hot note about 0.15. Nothing ever goes above 0.2 (pigment, never neon) or below where it was.
  • Amount (0 to 1): how much of the effect right now. It comes from the intensity metric (the ride’s speed and hits, the same for every song), through a curve: below.
  • Strength: how far the effect goes at full amount. With 1.1 a paper’s colour nearly doubles (× 1.94), bars gain half again (× 1.55), a hot note a fifth (× 1.22). With 0.75: × 1.64, × 1.38, × 1.15.
  • The paper: changes like the rest, half as much, or stays exactly as designed (the pencilled course ahead goes with it).

It is done on the theme’s own colours, not on the pixels, so it is exact for flat colour, and the rider (the original artwork) is never touched. Round 33’s and 34’s colours are reproduced exactly (the stills are byte-identical).

The curves

How often it is on

The share of each song’s time with any of the effect (more than 2 %), and with at least half of it, by where it starts. The intensity is absolute, so an intense song gets more of it: that is the rule we chose for everything (never a song’s own percentiles), but it means “only when intense” is most of the song on Batidão.
