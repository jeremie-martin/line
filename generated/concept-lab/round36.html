Round thirty-six · 9 November

The intensity, exactly

Before tuning vibrance’s curve: what the intensity is, how it is computed, why it stays between about 0.2 and 0.85, how fast it reacts, and what we could change. Everything here is measured on the 22 production rides the metric was tuned on.

How it is computed

  1. Two measures of the ride, ten times a second. Speed: how fast the rider goes. Impacts: every hit’s measured strength (what the ride actually did, not what was asked; 0 below 0.2, 1 from 0.85, curved), added up per second. So impacts are how often × how hard: two strong hits a second count like four medium ones.
  2. “Right now”: speed averaged over 0.3 s; each hit spread over about a second around its moment.
  3. Steadied: a 6-second sliding median (it ignores anything shorter than 3 s, up or down, yet keeps a real change sharp), then softened by a 1-second blur. No lag: the whole ride is known, so it is centred on the moment, not delayed.
  4. Scaled, the same for every song: each measure is 0 at the 5th percentile of the 22 rides and 1 at their 99.5th (speed 334 to 493 units a second; impacts 0.16 to 1.41 strength a second), cut at 0 and 1.
  5. Combined: the average of the two.
  6. No flashes: it never changes by more than 0.3 a second, so from 0 to 1 takes at least 3.3 seconds.

In round 4 you said: speed plus impact, impact meaning how often and how hard; speed one of the biggest contributors, “on the condition that there are impacts”. In the blind test it agreed with all 8 of your clear answers (the 5 “same” were not counted).

Your questions

Why never above 1, and why does it stop near 0.8? Not luck: by construction. Each half is cut at 1, but the cut almost never acts: without it, the average goes above 1 less than 0.1 % of the time. It stops near 0.8 because the two halves rarely peak together (the fastest moments are dives, between hits). Over the 22 rides half of all time is above 0.60, a quarter above 0.73, 1 % above 0.85. So the plateau at 0.8 in round 35 was the curve’s choice, not the metric’s: a curve should keep rising across what actually happens, up to about 0.9.

The curve

Your idea: nothing at the very bottom, then a slow start, then faster, the corners smoothed. Two such curves, beside round 35’s, over where the 22 rides spend their time (the bars):

  • Soft start: nothing up to 0.3; a slow rise to 15 % at 0.6 (the median moment); then faster, to 100 % at 0.9.
  • Softer: nothing up to 0.35; 8 % at 0.65; 100 % at 0.92.

Both pass through their points with a smooth monotone curve (no overshoot, no corner), and keep rising across everything that happens: only about 1 % of time is above 0.85.

What I propose

  1. Keep the metric’s calm (no flashes); see the quicker recipe in motion before changing it.
  2. Try “both needed”: it matches your words in both rounds, and changes little otherwise.
  3. Drive vibrance with a soft-start curve that rises to about 0.9.
  4. Then a lab round: the intensity drawn over the ride (as in round 4), and vibrance on the new curve, with today’s and the quicker recipe side by side.
