The camera, measured

I measured how the rider moves in the frame against how it moves in the world, on all seven new rides. Two numbers matter most:

CameraRider rises on screen while going downRider height (median)
Today’s official camera5.1 s per minute44 %
Tamed1.8 s per minute43 %
Higher1.8 s per minute38 %
Follows direction0 s per minute38 %

Follows direction is one rule for everything. The rider’s height on screen follows the slope it rides, growing with the slope’s square, so a 60° ride sits clearly lower than a 35° one. While the rider descends, it may rise on screen only slowly (45 px/s). It is also calmer overall: 59 px/s of on-screen motion against Tamed’s 94. At your two moments it lowers the rider by 5.5 % and 4.0 % of the screen, against Tamed’s 3.7 % and 2.1 %.

A narrower rule I tried first, dropping the rider only on steep rides, moved more at your moment (6 %). But it made the rider climb back during descents (5 s per minute), so I left it out. The full table is in analysis/camstats.txt.

Scheme, style, step: how the pieces fit

You asked for my view on this, so it has its own page: How the pieces fit. In short, there are four kinds of things. Signals are what is true about the ride. Elements are the instruments. Schemes are colours only. Direction is the choices for one video, where intent lives. A look is only a saved direction. Every comparison should name what it varies, and your verdicts are now filed per piece in a ledger (also LEDGER.md in the lab), so feedback on a palette is never mistaken for feedback on a video.

Timing

On the old rides, the rider’s first touch lands within −6 to +10 ms of the sound’s rise, under one video frame. So syncing the visuals to the touch or to the music onset is currently the same thing, and the lab fires accents at the touch. The −15 ms jolt is a compiler constant, and whether it’s still right is the compiler track’s question. I wrote the other agent a brief with your questions and my measurements: agents/jolt-brief.md.
